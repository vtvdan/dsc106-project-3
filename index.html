Polar Amplification: Latitudinal Asymmetry in Projected Surface Warming

The Arctic is warming much faster than the rest of the planet, a pattern called polar amplification. Pick an emissions scenario and move the year to see how much faster. Hover the chart to read any latitude band.

Each point on the line is a band of latitude (north is up). The farther right it sits, the more that band has warmed since 1995-2014. The straight dashed vertical line is the global average, and the shaded area is the gap from it.

Write-up

Motivation

We chose this topic because of the stark difference in warming in the arctic compared to the rest of the world we found in our exploratory visualizations. We also recognized the significant impact arctic warming will have, leading to a rising sea level and other environmental effects. A viewer of this visualization should see how warming plays out over time and in different scenarios and see the particularly large impact on the arctic.

Design Decisions

We wanted our visualisation to convey how temperature would change over time, but we also wanted to show that latitude influenced the degree of warming. Since our data gives us temperature projections in each year at many latitudes, we decided to use a curved line plot displaying the projected temperature at each latitude. We decided to have latitude on the vertical axis rather than the horizontal axis because on a 2D map latitude is on the vertical axis. We decided to use different colors to represent the different emissions scenarios, escalating from blue to yellow to red to represent the change to a scenario with more warming. We also highlight areas below the lines to exaggerate the area. We included useful stats like the arctic's warming, the mean warming, and their ratio on the right side for the view to easily reference. To convey change over time, we use an interactive slider which changes the year from 2015-2100. This slider changes the graph, the description, and the statistics. We also wanted regions like the arctic and tropics to stand out on the graph, so those latitude ranges are slightly highlighted.
